## [Supreme Court Rules Geofence Warrants Unconstitutional Without Warrant](https://www.theguardian.com/us-news/2026/jun/29/supreme-court-geofence-warrants-case-decision) ⭐️ 9.0/10

The US Supreme Court ruled that geofence warrants, which allow law enforcement to obtain location data on all devices within a specific area and time, are unconstitutional without a warrant based on probable cause. This landmark decision establishes new Fourth Amendment protections for digital location data. The ruling limits law enforcement's ability to conduct dragnet surveillance, protecting individuals' privacy in the digital age. It sets a precedent that may influence how tech companies handle user location data and respond to government requests. In the case, Google provided location data in three tranches: first an anonymized list of devices near a crime scene, then upon further request, account identifiers and more. The Court emphasized that such searches intrude on reasonable expectations of privacy and require a warrant.

hackernews · cdrnsf · Jun 29, 15:54 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48720924)

**Background**: A geofence warrant is a court order that compels a company like Google to search its location database for all devices present in a defined geographic area during a specified time window. Law enforcement uses them to identify suspects when they lack a specific individual to investigate. This practice raised Fourth Amendment concerns because it can reveal the movements of many innocent people, effectively turning their digital footprints into evidence without individualized suspicion.

## [WATaBoy: JIT-Ing Game Boy Instructions to WASM Beats a Native Interpreter](https://humphri.es/blog/WATaBoy/) ⭐️ 8.0/10

The WATaBoy project demonstrates that just-in-time compiling Game Boy CPU instructions to WebAssembly within a browser can outperform a traditional native interpreter. It dynamically translates each instruction into a WebAssembly module at runtime, leveraging the browser's own JIT for further optimization. This technique enables near-native performance for emulators on the web, opening the door to running demanding retro games directly in browsers without plugins. It also creatively circumvents platform JIT restrictions, such as those on iOS, by exploiting the browser's exemption for JavaScript and WebAssembly. The emulator compiles each Game Boy instruction into a dedicated WebAssembly function, which the browser's JIT then further compiles to machine code, yielding a speedup over pure interpretation. On Firefox, performance is about 25% slower than on Chrome or Safari, highlighting browser-specific optimization differences.

hackernews · energeticbark · Jun 29, 15:02 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48720190)

**Background**: WebAssembly is a low-level binary format that runs at near-native speed in modern browsers. Traditional emulators use interpreters, which are slow because they execute instructions sequentially. Just-in-time (JIT) compilation translates instructions to machine code at runtime for better performance. On iOS, apps cannot use JIT, but browsers are allowed to JIT-compile JavaScript and WebAssembly, providing a loophole.
