## [Tao explains Jacobian conjecture counterexample](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2026/07/21/a-digestion-of-the-jacobian-conjecture-counterexample/) ⭐️ 9.0/10

Terry Tao published a July 21, 2026 explanation of a purported counterexample to the Jacobian conjecture, digesting the construction and why its cancellations are surprising. The broader claim is that Levent Alpöge presented an explicit three-dimensional counterexample on July 19, 2026. If correct, this would overturn a major long-standing problem in algebraic geometry and commutative algebra for dimensions greater than two. Tao’s exposition matters because it makes a highly technical claimed breakthrough more checkable and understandable to mathematicians and technically curious readers. The conjecture says that a polynomial map from an N-dimensional space to itself with a nonzero constant Jacobian determinant should have a polynomial inverse. The reported counterexample is in three variables and involves a degree-seven polynomial map whose Jacobian determinant unexpectedly collapses to a constant after many possible higher-degree terms cancel.

hackernews · jeremyscanvic · Jul 21, 21:09 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48998362)

**Background**: The Jacobian determinant measures how a multivariable function locally changes volume, and in this conjecture it is applied to polynomial maps. The Jacobian conjecture was first stated for two variables by Ludwig Kraus in 1884 and later restated by Ott-Heinrich Keller in 1939 for polynomial maps with integer coefficients. It became famous because it is easy to state using ideas close to calculus, but has resisted proof for more than a century and appears on Stephen Smale’s 1998 list of mathematical problems. According to the search results, the two-variable case remains unresolved as of July 2026 even if the claimed higher-dimensional counterexample is accepted.

## [Judge approves Anthropic’s $1.5B book settlement.](https://apnews.com/article/ai-anthropic-copyright-settlement-claude-books-bartz-74b140444023898aeba8579b6e9f0d63) ⭐️ 8.0/10

A judge approved Anthropic’s $1.5 billion settlement in a copyright class action over pirated books allegedly used in connection with training Claude. The case is notable because earlier rulings distinguished Anthropic’s liability for acquiring pirated books from the separate fair-use question of training large language models on books. The settlement creates a major financial precedent for AI companies that obtained copyrighted works from unauthorized sources, even where model training itself may receive fair-use protection. Authors, publishers, AI labs, and data brokers will likely treat the outcome as a warning about provenance, licensing, and recordkeeping for training datasets. Community discussion highlighted that eligible titles are expected to receive about $3,000 each, and that the judge reportedly reduced class counsel fees from 12.5% to 6.8%. The key caveat is that the settlement should not be read as a blanket ruling that LLM training on copyrighted books is unlawful; the core liability at issue was tied to pirated acquisition.

hackernews · BeetleB · Jul 21, 19:04 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48996652)

**Background**: Claude is Anthropic’s family of large language models, which are trained on large text datasets to predict and generate language. Copyright disputes over LLM training often turn on fair use, a U.S. doctrine that can permit certain unlicensed uses depending on factors such as purpose, transformation, market harm, and the nature of the work. Recent AI copyright cases have increasingly separated two questions: whether training on a work is fair use, and whether the company lawfully obtained the copy it used. That distinction matters because a company may have a stronger fair-use argument for analysis or training than for downloading or retaining pirated source material.
