## [First Synthetic Cell Built from Scratch Grows and Divides](https://www.quantamagazine.org/for-the-first-time-a-cell-built-from-scratch-grows-and-divides-20260701/) ⭐️ 10.0/10

Scientists led by Dr. Kate Adamala at the University of Minnesota have created SpudCell, a synthetic cell built entirely from laboratory chemicals that can grow by fusing with droplets, replicate its DNA, and divide into daughter cells, completing a full life cycle for the first time. This breakthrough overcomes a major obstacle in synthetic biology—achieving controlled cell division without a natural cytoskeleton—bringing us closer to building life from scratch and enabling potential applications in medicine, biotechnology, and bioengineering. The SpudCell design abandons the cytoskeleton, instead using a novel division mechanism, with genes borrowed from a virus and E. coli; the 190-page manuscript was initially sent to journalists under embargo before peer review and is now available on bioRxiv.

**Background**: Synthetic biologists have long sought to build artificial cells that mimic life's functions. Previous efforts yielded cells that could grow and replicate DNA, but cell division—a complex process involving cytoskeleton reorganization—remained elusive. SpudCell represents the first bottom-up synthetic cell to complete a full life cycle using only chemical components.

## [FFmpeg 9.1's Improved AAC Encoder](https://hydrogenaudio.org/index.php/topic,129691.0.html) ⭐️ 7.0/10

FFmpeg 9.1 introduces a significantly improved AAC encoder that addresses previous quality issues, but it is currently limited to constant bitrate (CBR) mode and optimized for 48kHz sampling. This upgrade resolves long-standing audio quality problems in FFmpeg's AAC encoder, which produced artifacts and inferior sound, benefiting users who rely on FFmpeg for multimedia encoding and potentially reducing the need for external encoders like Apple's Core Audio. The encoder is CBR-only, lacks quality-based variable bitrate (VBR) support, and is mainly optimized for 48kHz, though 44.1kHz will work; it also fixes a stereo PNS decoder bug, a feature previously unused by other encoders, which had remained unnoticed.

hackernews · ledoge · Jul 1, 14:10 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48747116)

**Background**: FFmpeg is a widely-used open-source multimedia framework for encoding, decoding, and transcoding audio and video. AAC (Advanced Audio Coding) is a lossy audio compression standard commonly used in MP4 videos. The previous FFmpeg AAC encoder was known for poor quality and chirping artifacts, leading many users to switch to alternatives like Fraunhofer FDK AAC or Apple's Core Audio encoder.

**Discussion**: Community reactions are mixed: some applaud the improvement but criticize the lack of VBR and 44.1kHz optimization as major gaps; others compare it unfavorably to Opus at low bitrates; there is also appreciation for the fix of a long-standing PNS decoder bug, and debate over whether 48kHz is truly the modern standard.

Meta's announcement of its AI cloud strategy caused a sharp decline in the stock prices of major semiconductor companies, including Nvidia, AMD, Intel, and Micron. The market reaction signals potential shifts in AI hardware demand, which could reshape the competitive landscape of the semiconductor industry and affect investor confidence in AI-driven growth. The stock drop appears driven by fears that Meta may reduce its reliance on external chip suppliers, though no specific technical details about the cloud strategy were reported.

openbb · AMD · Jul 1, 20:03

**Background**: Nvidia, AMD, Intel, and Micron are key providers of GPUs, CPUs, and memory chips essential for AI training and inference. Meta has been a major customer, investing heavily in data centers powered by these components. Any strategic shift by Meta towards in-house chips or alternative cloud architectures could significantly impact these companies' revenues.
