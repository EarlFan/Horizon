## [NEvo evolves videos to target brain regions.](https://nevo-project.epfl.ch/) ⭐️ 8.0/10

EPFL's NEvo project introduces Neural-Guided Evolutionary Video Synthesis, a framework that uses AI-generated videos and brain encoding models to find stimuli predicted to maximally activate a chosen visual region of interest. The arXiv paper, listed as 2607.02317, describes candidate prompts being rendered into videos, scored by a predicted brain response, and iteratively refined through evolutionary search. This matters because it moves stimulus optimization from mostly static images toward naturalistic dynamic videos, which are closer to how humans normally perceive the world. It could help neuroscientists probe what different visual regions respond to with less hand-designed experimenter bias, while also raising concerns about future systems that personalize media to manipulate attention or emotion. According to the project and paper summary, NEvo operates in silico: it relies on a learned brain encoding model, sometimes described as a digital twin, to predict activation rather than directly stimulating the brain during every search step. A key caveat raised by readers is validation: predicted maximal activation is only scientifically meaningful if the synthesized videos are later tested against measured brain responses.

hackernews · smusamashah · Jul 10, 07:39 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48856904)

**Background**: A brain encoding model is a computational model that predicts how a brain region will respond to a stimulus, such as an image or video. In visual neuroscience, researchers use such models to study selectivity, meaning which kinds of visual input a region responds to most strongly. Earlier related work has often optimized static images or preferred stimuli for visual neurons, while NEvo focuses on dynamic visual input and videos. The project describes this as a way to map how visual selectivity becomes more dynamic and social along the lateral visual stream.
