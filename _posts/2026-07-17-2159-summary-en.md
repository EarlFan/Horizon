## [EEG reveals dual speech-stream encoding.](https://journals.plos.org/plosbiology/article?id=10.1371/journal.pbio.3003876) ⭐️ 6.0/10

A new PLOS Biology study reports EEG evidence that the human cortex can transiently represent two competing speech streams during attention switching. The study found that neural tracking of the new target speech stream can emerge before tracking of the previous target fully disengages. The finding refines the common view that attention simply selects one speech stream while suppressing the other. It may inform research on auditory attention, multitalker listening, EEG-based attention decoding, and cognitive models of how people handle competing speech. The reported effect appears to be transient and tied to attention switching, not evidence that listeners fully comprehend two conversations at once without cost. The PLOS Biology abstract describes an asymmetric process: engagement with the new target arises before disengagement from the old target is complete.

hackernews · giuliomagnifico · Jul 17, 05:51 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48943745)

**Background**: EEG, or electroencephalography, measures electrical activity from the scalp and is often used to study how the brain responds to sound over time. In speech research, “neural tracking” usually means that measured brain signals are statistically related to features of a speech signal, such as its acoustic or linguistic structure. Prior work has used models such as temporal response functions to compare predicted EEG responses with actual EEG responses, treating stronger prediction as evidence that the brain is tracking a stimulus.

## [Roman Latrine Reveals Concrete Durability Clues.](https://www.smithsonianmag.com/smart-news/how-has-roman-concrete-lasted-for-millennia-a-1900-year-old-latrine-offers-new-clues-about-the-materials-impressive-durability-180989115/) ⭐️ 6.0/10

A study of a roughly 1,900-year-old Roman latrine suggests that ongoing carbonation reactions helped Roman concrete keep densifying and sealing cracks over centuries. The work adds carbonation to the better-known pozzolanic reaction between lime and volcanic ash as a contributor to the material’s long-term durability. Understanding why some ancient concrete survived for millennia could inform more durable modern cementitious materials and lower the repair burden for infrastructure. It also reframes Roman concrete as a chemically evolving material rather than simply a static ancient recipe. The reported evidence centers on calcium carbonate networks formed through mineralized carbonation, observed with advanced multiscale imaging such as spectroscopy and tomography. A key caveat is that Roman concrete was not the same as modern reinforced concrete, whose lifetime is often limited by steel rebar corrosion as well as by the concrete matrix itself.

hackernews · divbzero · Jul 17, 03:48 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48943142)

**Background**: Roman concrete commonly used lime-based binders and volcanic materials known as pozzolans. In the lime cycle, calcium compounds can transform from quicklime or slaked lime toward calcium carbonate as they react with carbon dioxide. Carbonation can harden and densify lime-based materials, while pozzolanic reactions can help them set and gain strength in wet environments. These processes are relevant because Roman structures often lack the steel reinforcement that creates a major corrosion pathway in many modern concrete structures.
