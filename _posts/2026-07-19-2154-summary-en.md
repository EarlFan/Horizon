## [Transcribe.cpp brings local STT to C/C++](https://workshop.cjpais.com/projects/transcribe-cpp) ⭐️ 7.0/10

Transcribe.cpp is a new open-source C/C++ speech-to-text inference library based on ggml, with support for more than 16 model families. Mozilla.ai announced it as part of its Builders in Residence program, emphasizing portable, GPU-accelerated local transcription. The project could make it easier for developers to embed fast, local speech recognition into desktop apps, dictation tools, captioning systems, and domain-specific workflows. Its local-first design is especially relevant as developers seek alternatives to cloud STT services for privacy, latency, cost, and offline use. The project page says models published under the handy-computer Hugging Face organization have been numerically validated and WER-tested to match their reference implementations. Community comments also highlight practical gaps to watch, including continuous low-latency dictation, easier binary releases, IPA-style phonetic transcription, and long-term maintenance funding.

hackernews · sebjones · Jul 19, 00:38 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48963879)

**Background**: Speech-to-text systems convert spoken audio into written text, and they are commonly used for dictation, captions, meeting notes, and accessibility features. ggml is a lightweight machine-learning inference framework often used to run models efficiently on local hardware. Word error rate, or WER, is a common metric for evaluating transcription accuracy by comparing predicted text with a reference transcript. Running STT locally can reduce dependence on network connectivity and cloud APIs, but it also places more importance on device performance and model optimization.

A software engineer published a retrospective after selling 2,500 JamCorder MIDI recording devices, arguing that the physical hardware work was smoother than expected. The story is useful for software engineers and indie founders considering physical products because it challenges the assumption that hardware is necessarily the hardest part. According to the discussion excerpt, JamCorder involved roughly 200,000 lines of code across firmware, an app, and manufacturing tooling, developed over more than three years. The product records standard MIDI files to a card, which reduces dependence on a long-lived proprietary app for accessing recordings.

hackernews · chipweinberger · Jul 19, 10:34 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48966713)

**Background**: MIDI, short for Musical Instrument Digital Interface, is a technical standard for connecting electronic musical instruments, computers, and related audio devices. It represents musical performance information as digital messages, such as notes and control changes, rather than as recorded audio waveforms. A MIDI recorder therefore captures performance data that can later be edited, replayed, or imported into music software.
