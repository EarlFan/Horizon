## [Relativity reshapes heavy-element chemical bonds.](https://www.brown.edu/news/2026-07-09/chemical-bonds-relativity) ⭐️ 6.0/10

A new Science paper highlighted by Brown University reports that relativistic spin-orbit coupling significantly changes how sigma and pi bonds behave in heavy-element molecules. The work focuses on bismuth, a heavy element near lead, and uses near-absolute-zero experiments and photoelectron spectroscopy to probe the bonding. The result helps clarify how Einsteinian relativity enters ordinary-looking chemistry when nuclei become very heavy. It could improve how chemists model heavy-element compounds, which matters for materials science and for understanding the limits of familiar bonding pictures taught for lighter elements. The key mechanism is spin-orbit coupling: in the relativistic regime, an electron’s spin and orbital motion are no longer independent. The standard picture in which one stronger sigma bond is surrounded by weaker side-by-side pi bonds works well for lighter elements, but the Brown summary says this picture becomes more complicated toward the bottom of the periodic table.

hackernews · hhs · Jul 10, 22:30 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48866134)

**Background**: Relativistic quantum chemistry studies cases where electrons move fast enough that nonrelativistic approximations become inaccurate. In heavy elements, the large nuclear charge can make inner electrons move at a significant fraction of the speed of light, changing orbital energies and bonding behavior. Sigma and pi bonds are common ways to describe how atomic orbitals overlap in molecules, especially in multiple bonds. Spin-orbit coupling is one relativistic effect in which an electron’s intrinsic spin interacts with its motion around the nucleus.

## [Crypto prepares for quantum risks.](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/crypto/articles/crypto-industry-steps-quantum-security-130000468.html) ⭐️ 4.0/10

The crypto industry is reportedly stepping up preparations for possible quantum-computing attacks against today’s encryption and digital-signature systems. The article frames this as an accelerating security concern rather than a single new technical breakthrough. Blockchains rely heavily on public-key cryptography to prove asset ownership and authorize transactions, so a practical quantum attack could threaten wallets, exchanges, and long-lived on-chain assets. The issue also reflects a broader cybersecurity shift toward post-quantum cryptography before large-scale quantum computers become operational. The main technical concern is that quantum algorithms such as Shor’s algorithm could undermine elliptic-curve cryptography, including signature schemes used by many crypto networks. Migration is difficult because blockchains are decentralized, immutable systems where changing cryptographic primitives requires broad coordination and compatibility planning.

openbb · AMD · Jul 11, 13:00

**Background**: Post-quantum cryptography refers to cryptographic algorithms designed to remain secure against attacks by future quantum computers. Many blockchains use elliptic-curve-based public-key systems, where users sign transactions to prove control of funds. Research on post-quantum blockchain cryptography argues that both public-key cryptography and, to a lesser extent, hash-based components may need reassessment as quantum capabilities improve. Because blockchain data and account histories are persistent, the migration problem is not just a software update but a long-term protocol and governance challenge.
